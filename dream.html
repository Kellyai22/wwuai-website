The full interview, five stages, one paste

Your dream Tuesday, the work that funds it, the honest numbers, where you actually are now, and the bridge. One question at a time, and it waits for your answer before moving on.

The prompt
I want you to interview me to uncover the life I actually want, then design the bridge from where I am now to that life. I'm a woman in my 40s or 50s and I have a rough picture of my dream life, but I keep daydreaming instead of acting, and I want that to stop. Run this as a proper interview. One question (or one small set) at a time. Wait for my answer before moving on. Push me when I'm vague. If I give you a highlight reel, ask for the detail underneath it. If I skip a question, gently point it out and ask again, because the questions I dodge are usually the ones that matter. Work through these five stages in order: STAGE 1. THE DREAM TUESDAY Ask me to describe a random Tuesday in my dream life, cinematically, from the moment I open my eyes. What time is it, where am I, who's there, what do I do in the first two hours, how does my body feel, what am I no longer doing? Push for specifics: the drink, the room, the order of things. Then take me through the rest of the day. STAGE 2. THE ENGINE Ask me what funds that Tuesday. Not the tasks, the shape of the work. What am I known for? What am I selling and to whom? What have I stopped doing entirely? What lands in my inbox on that Tuesday? If my dream day was vivid but my answer here is fuzzy, tell me so, because that gap is usually why the dream stays a dream. STAGE 3. THE HONEST NUMBERS Ask me what the dream life actually costs per year, and help me separate one-off capital costs (like a house) from annual living costs. Challenge my numbers kindly if they seem inflated or bundled together. Ask what my household actually earns and spends now. Ask whether the dream is the lifestyle itself or the expensive version of it, because those can be very different distances away. STAGE 4. THE CURRENT-STATE AUDIT Ask me, without judgement: What time do I actually get up, and what do the first two hours really look like? What is genuinely coming in financially from my own work right now? What habit or struggle keeps repeating (health, food, phone, avoidance), and what would 'won' look like to me in my own words? Whose schedule or needs am I letting shape my days? Notice anything from my dream Tuesday that hasn't appeared in my answers here, and ask me about it. STAGE 5. THE BRIDGE Now design my plan. Keep it brutally simple, four things maximum: 1. A wake time, drawn from my dream morning, not from someone else's 5am rule 2. A morning routine built only from pieces of my dream morning that are available to me now, in the right order 3. One daily non-negotiable action that moves my biggest gap (usually money or health), done before anything else 4. One thing to remove or stop deciding daily, to take willpower out of the equation Tell me plainly what to ignore for the next 90 days. Do not give me a 20-item habit list. Do not let me add projects. If my plan depends on things I said I can't afford, redesign it with free alternatives. Throughout the whole interview: be warm but honest. Don't flatter me. If you spot me talking myself into comfort or out of action, name the pattern kindly and keep going. At the end, summarise my dream Tuesday in one paragraph so I can read it every morning. Start with Stage 1 now.

One last thing. The point of this isn't the plan. Plans are easy. The point is the moment, somewhere around the honest numbers or the current-state audit, where you write down something true that you've been walking around. That's where the bridge actually starts.
