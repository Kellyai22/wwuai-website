Your content lives in one place: your head

29 July 2026 · By Kelly Hartley · Wise Women Use AI

You know the moment. You sit down to post something, and there's nothing there. Just the blinking cursor and that small, sinking question: what do I even say today?

So you scroll for a bit, hoping something will spark. You half-start a caption, delete it, start again. And somewhere in there, the morning you'd set aside for your actual business quietly disappears.

I want to say something to you, because I think you've been carrying it as a personal failing when it was never one.

The problem isn't that you're bad at content. It's that your content has never had anywhere to live except inside your own head.

Every idea, every good line you thought of in the shower, every "I should post about that", all of it held in the one place that's already holding everything else. The client work. The invoices. The appointment you almost forgot. The word that was on the tip of your tongue an hour ago and then just wasn't. And now the content too, expected to appear, fresh and clever, out of a brain that's already full, and some days working through brain fog.

No wonder the blank page feels so heavy. You're not starting from nothing. You're starting from everything, all at once, with nowhere to put any of it down.

And here's the part almost nobody says to you. The reason it all lives in your head is that, for years, your head was the only place it could go. You've been the whole team. The ideas person, and the writer, and the one who schedules it, and the one who remembers what worked last time. Of course it's all in there. Where else was it meant to live? You never had a filing cabinet or an assistant or a system. You had you, and you made it work, because that is what you do.

Let me show you what I mean, because I think you'll recognise every one of these.

There's the idea that arrives at the worst possible time. You're driving, or doing the dishes, or half asleep, and it's a good one, genuinely. And by the time you're at your desk with a spare few minutes, it's gone. Not because you're forgetful. Because there was nowhere to catch it.

There's the guilt of going quiet. You post consistently for a couple of weeks, feel good about it, and then life happens and you vanish for a month. And the silence starts to feel like proof that you're just not cut out for this. You are. Consistency was never a personality trait you're missing. It's a system nobody ever handed you.

There's the pressure to be everywhere. Instagram, and Facebook, and now apparently Threads, and you're one woman trying to run an actual business, not a media company. So you spread yourself thin across all of it, and it all feels a bit rubbish, and you wonder how everyone else makes it look so effortless.

They're not more clever than you, by the way. They just stopped trying to hold it all in their head. That's the whole difference.

And then there's Sunday. The evening is technically yours, but next week's content has already started reading itself out to you. Nothing written, no plan, just the low hum of it circling. It isn't the posting that's heavy. It's carrying a whole week of it in your head with no idea where any of it is going to come from.

If even one of those made you exhale, I want you to know: it's not you. It's the setup. And setups can be changed.

Here's the thing I've watched shift for women in our community, over and over. The moment the content stops living in your head and starts living in one real place, the whole thing gets lighter. Not because you're posting more. Because you're carrying less.

That is what I want to help you start.

On Wednesday the 12th of August, I'm running a free workshop. One hour. And in that hour, we are going to build the one place your content lives, together, while you talk.

Here's how it actually works, because I don't run the kind of workshop where I talk at you for an hour and you leave with a page of notes and nothing built.

You'll connect Claude and Notion, and I'll walk you through it on the day. Then Claude interviews you, gently, about your business. What you do, who it's for, the questions your customers always ask. And out of that conversation, it surfaces a first pass at your content pillars. The handful of things you're actually here to talk about, named and on the page, instead of swirling around in your head.

Then we build a simple home for them. A one-page Content Command Centre in Notion, with a calendar you can keep even on a bad week. We'll tag one pillar and fill your first few days together, so you leave with something real. Not a blank page. A start.

I want to be honest with you about what it is and what it isn't, because I never want you turning up expecting the wrong thing. You won't leave with a finished content system, all singing and dancing. What you'll leave with is the first real piece of it, built by you, sitting in one place you own. The kind of start you can actually build on, instead of another half-finished thing on the list you never get back to.

You don't need to be techy. You don't need your business all figured out first, or to tidy anything up before you arrive. You just need to be a little bit tired of carrying it all in your head.

Bring a cup of tea. Camera off if you'd rather. There's no hot seat, no homework, nothing to prepare. Just show up, and we'll build the first piece together. If you'd like a Claude account and a Notion account ready so you can build along, brilliant. And if you'd rather just watch this time and set yours up after, that's completely fine too. You'll get the step-by-step either way.

Picture the Sunday after. Next week's posts already sitting in one place, roughed out, waiting. You glance at them, maybe change a line, and then you close the laptop and the evening is actually yours. No circling. No hum. The week is on the page, not on your chest. That isn't a fantasy. It's just what happens when the content has somewhere to live that isn't you.

This is the advantage we finally have, the one women running businesses on their own never used to get. A second pair of hands that doesn't cost a salary. Somewhere for the content to live that isn't the inside of your head. And the point of it isn't to do more. It's to get some of your head back, so there's room again for the part of the business you actually love.

You were never meant to hold all of it, all the time. And after the 12th, you won't have to hold the content part on your own any more.

Come and build the first piece with me. It's free, it's an hour, and you'll leave with a real start.
