It was never a discipline problem

13 July 2026 · By Kelly Hartley · Wise Women Use AI

For years I thought I was just bad at staying organised.

That was the story I told myself, and I told it for a long time. Everyone else seemed to have it together. I was the one holding a whole business up with sticky notes, a memory that was already full, and about fourteen tabs open at any given time. If I could just be a bit more disciplined, a bit more on top of things, surely it would settle down.

It never settled down.

I've started and quietly closed more businesses than I'd happily admit to at a dinner party. I trained as an interior designer, sure I'd build something of my own. I did build one, back in the UK, and then the GFC arrived and it didn't survive it. After that came a run of side ventures I squeezed in around a full-time job and a young family, late at night, in every spare hour I could find. A bit of dropshipping. Some e-commerce. Small marketing projects.

None of them fell over because the ideas were bad. They fell over because there was only one of me, and every single one of them lived entirely in my head.

And a head doesn't get a day off.

You know this feeling, I think. It's the 3am wake-up, suddenly remembering the thing you meant to send. It's standing in the kitchen at 6pm being asked what's for dinner while you're still mentally drafting a reply to a client. It's the holiday where you checked the inbox by the pool, just the once, just in case, because you're the only one who actually knows how any of it works.

We call that being committed. A safe pair of hands. Really it's a business, and a home, and a family all running through one person's head at once, with no off switch, because she can't switch off either.

And it isn't only the business, is it. It's the dentist appointments and the school forms and the what's-for-dinner, threaded through the same tired mind that's trying to run a company. One person holding every thread, hoping none of them slip.

I felt it most sharply in the job I had before this one. For a couple of years I was a buyer for an online store, though in truth I did the majority of everything else too. The website, the products, the ordering, the thousand small decisions a day. When I started, the business was a lot smaller, and as it grew, I grew with the role. Somewhere along the way I realised I understood how that side of it actually worked better than the owner probably did.

And I realised something else, something that stuck with me. Almost none of it was written down. It lived in my head, and only my head, and that is a fragile way for any business to run.

I'd already started looking at AI before I left. Quietly nerdy about tech, I'd been playing with ChatGPT, setting it up to handle the boring, repetitive jobs I never had time for, and quietly wondering about going out on my own again. So when I was let go late last year, as unsettling as it was, I didn't only see a door closing. I saw the sign to go all in.

Here's what landed properly. If I'd had these tools for every business I'd ever tried, the ones that quietly burned me out, I think I'd have gone so much further. Not because I'd have worked harder. I was already working every hour there was. Because I'd finally have had the help to close the gap between what I could see and what I could actually build.

That gap has been the story of my whole working life. I'm a big-picture person. I can see exactly what I want. Getting there, on my own, with no team and no budget, was always the hard part.

Here's what I wish someone had said to me twenty years ago. It was never a discipline problem. It was a setup problem.

The women whose businesses feel calm aren't more organised than you. They're not tidier, or more sorted, or better at mornings. Their businesses simply live somewhere other than their heads. One home the whole thing runs from. Not ten apps you half-use, not a notebook you lose, not a memory you're quietly terrified of dropping. One calm place where the work sits, so your brain doesn't have to hold it.

So the very first thing I built with my new set of hands wasn't clever or complicated. It was a home for the business. One place where it all lives now, so it stops living in me. The night I finished setting it up, I slept through for the first time in longer than I'd like to admit. Not because the work had got any smaller. Because I'd finally been able to put it down.
