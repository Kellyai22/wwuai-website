The quick version

Why this works when the usual review doesn't

Most of us skip the mid-year review because it feels like homework. Spreadsheets, judgement, a whole day you don't have. So the year keeps rolling and every win, worry and half-finished thing stays in your head, where it weighs the most. This one is different because you don't do the heavy lifting. You talk, Claude sorts. You decide, Claude writes it down. Four prompts, run in order, in one chat. You finish with a one-page plan for the rest of 2026, and that's the advantage we never used to have: a second brain that holds all of it, so yours doesn't have to.

One sitting, four steps

The four prompts

Open one new chat in Claude and run these in order, copy, paste, answer. Keep the same chat for all four so it remembers everything as you go.

1

Empty the half-year out of your head

Start here, and don't tidy anything first. You tell Claude everything you can remember about the year so far, in whatever order it comes out, and it does the sorting. If typing feels like work, tap the microphone and just talk. Rambling is allowed. Rambling is the point.

The prompt
I run my own business and I'm doing a calm mid-year review with you. This is step one: getting the last six months out of my head. I'm going to tell you everything I can remember about the year so far. The wins, the flops, the half-finished things, the clients, the money moments, the weeks that disappeared. Don't fix anything yet, just take it in. If I speak instead of type, treat the rambling exactly the same. When I'm done, sort what I said into four lists: what went well, what drained me, what I started and never finished, and what I keep saying I'll do but haven't. Then ask me up to five questions to fill the gaps, one at a time, and update the lists. Hold onto them, we'll use them for the rest of the review. Ready? Here's my half-year:
2

Sort what's working from what just looks busy

This is the step we never do on our own, because it means admitting some of the busyness produced nothing. Claude has no feelings about your Instagram grid or that offer you spent three weekends on, so it can say the quiet part kindly and plainly.

The prompt
Step two of my mid-year review. Using the four lists from step one, help me see what's actually working. Go through what went well and tell me what those things have in common: which kind of work, which kind of client, which offer, which price. Then look at everything else and be honest about what kept me busy without really producing money, energy or momentum. Give me two short lists: "more of this" and "this was noise". For anything on the noise list, tell me plainly why it's there. If you need rough numbers to judge something, ask me rather than guessing. Close enough is fine. This is a compass, not a tax return.
3

Decide what to drop, shrink or hand over

Here's where the headspace comes back. You're used to carrying all of it, so you'll defend things out of habit. This prompt asks Claude to push back gently, and to name what each thing actually costs you every week you keep it.

The prompt
Step three: the drop list. Take everything from this review so far and help me decide what I'll stop doing, shrink down, or hand over for the rest of the year. Push me a little here. I'm used to carrying all of it, so I'll defend things out of habit. For each thing you suggest dropping, tell me what it costs me each week, what would honestly happen if it stopped, and what I could do with the room it frees up. For anything that can't be dropped but drains me, suggest how AI could carry part of it. Be specific about what I'd hand over and what stays mine. I want to finish this step with at least three things gone, and the reasons written down so future me doesn't quietly pick them back up.
4

Point the next six months

The finish line. One focus for the rest of the year, milestones that fit around a real life, the very next action for this week, and the whole review written up as one page you keep. Six months from now, you read that page and do this again.

The prompt
Last step: point the next six months. From everything in this review, give me one clear focus for the rest of the year, the single thing that would make the biggest difference if it got most of my attention. Then break it down: what the next three months look like, then the three after that, with a handful of milestones I can actually hit around a real life. Family, the trips already booked, the lot. Under the first milestone, give me the very next action for this week, small enough to do in under an hour. Then write the whole review up as one page I can keep: what worked, what I dropped, my focus, my milestones and my next action. Dated, warm, in my words, no corporate speak. That page is the whole point. Six months from now I want to read it and do this again.

The review is one sitting. The system is the year.
