Why Claude keeps guessing about your business

  • You paste your bio in. Again.And your offer, and who it's for, every time you start a new chat.
  • The email sounds like someone else.It's polite enough, but it's nothing like the way you'd actually say it.
  • Last month's price turns up.It's working from an old chat where things were different.
  • Fixing the draft takes longer than writing it.So you start wondering if it's worth it at all.

Ordinary chats are fine for one-off questions. A quick lookup, a bit of brainstorming, what to cook tonight. And if you have memory switched on, Claude may already know a few things about you.

Your recurring business work is different. Your emails, your offers, your content, your customer replies. When that work is spread across dozens of scattered chats, Claude is piecing your business together from fragments, and some of those fragments are out of date. So it fills the gaps with guesses, and you end up explaining yourself again.

A Project fixes that. It gives Claude a deliberate, current brief on your business: who you are, who you serve, what you sell and how you sound, written down on purpose and kept up to date. Every chat you start inside it begins from that brief.

This page walks you through setting one up properly.

Your step-by-step

Set up your Project in five steps

0 of 5 done
  1. 1

    See the difference first

    Before you set anything up, open a normal chat and paste this in:

    The test
    Write me a short email to my list inviting them to my next offer.

    Save the answer somewhere. You'll come back to it at the end.

  2. 2

    Create your Project

    In Claude, go to Projects and create a new one. Give it your business name. That's it for now. Don't worry about the instructions box yet, because Claude is going to write those for you.

  3. 3

    Let Claude interview you

    Open a chat inside your new Project and paste this prompt:

    The interview prompt
    I want you to help me set up this Project so you understand my business properly. Interview me one question at a time. Cover who I am, what my business does, who my customers are and what they struggle with, what I sell and what it costs, how I like to sound when I write, and anything you should never do or say on my behalf. Keep your questions short. If one of my answers is vague, ask a follow-up before moving on. When you have enough, write me a set of Project instructions I can paste straight into the instructions box. Write them in plain language, in my voice, and keep them under 500 words.

    Answer as if you were talking to a new assistant on their first day. Messy answers are fine. Claude will tidy them up.

    When it gives you the instructions, copy them into your Project's instructions box and save.

  4. 4

    Add your documents

    Upload a few things Claude can refer back to. You don't need everything, just enough to give it a real picture:

    • Your offers or price list
    • Your about page or bio
    • Two or three emails or posts you've written that sound like you
    • Your most common customer questions, even as a rough list
    • Anything you find yourself explaining over and over

    If it lives in your head, a quick voice note turned into text is enough.

  5. 5

    Run the same test again

    Start a new chat inside your Project and paste the exact same request from Step 1:

    The test
    Write me a short email to my list inviting them to my next offer.

    Put the two answers side by side. The first one is working from whatever Claude happens to know. The second one is working from your brief, and it should sound like your business.

    That's the difference context makes.

Keep it working

A Project is only as good as what's in it. When something changes, like a new offer, a new price or a new kind of customer, update the documents. If Claude gets something wrong, tell it, then add the correction to the instructions so it stays fixed.

One rule worth keeping: one-off questions can go anywhere, but recurring business work belongs inside the Project.

Questions

Before you start

What is a Claude Project?

A Project is a workspace inside Claude with its own instructions and its own uploaded files. Every chat you start inside it begins from that brief, so Claude already knows who you are, who you serve, what you sell and how you sound before you type a word.

Do I need a paid Claude plan?

No. Projects are available on every Claude plan, including the free one, which lets you create up to five. That's plenty for this.

I already have memory switched on. Do I still need this?

Memory picks things up from your chats as you go, which is handy. A Project is different because you decide what goes in it, on purpose, and you keep it current. For recurring business work, that deliberate brief is what stops the guessing.

I'm not techy. Will I manage?

Yes. You copy a prompt, answer Claude's questions the way you'd talk to a new assistant, and paste what it writes back into one box. Messy answers are fine.

My Projects screen looks different. What now?

Claude updates its screens from time to time, and some accounts see a newer version of Projects. The idea stays the same: look for where your Project keeps its instructions and its files, and put the interview answers and your documents there.
