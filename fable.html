The work it's best at

The four jobs I'd give it

Copy each prompt, paste it into Claude Code or Cowork with Fable 5 selected, and let it work.

1

Hand it your big goal and get the plan

Start here. Give it the goal you actually care about this year, the revenue, the launch, the thing you keep not getting to, and let it reverse engineer the path with the next move marked. A smaller model guesses. This one reads your whole business first.

The prompt
Here's the big goal I'm holding: [write your goal here, with the deadline and any numbers that matter, for example the revenue, the launch, the milestone]. Reverse engineer it from there. First read everything you can see about my business and the way I work, so the plan fits me and not a generic template. Then map the path: the milestones between here and the goal, in order, with rough timing. Under each milestone, the specific actions, and which ones are mine to do versus which ones you or my other tools could take off my plate. Flag the two or three things most likely to trip me up and what I'd do about each. End with the very next action I should take this week. Keep it honest. If the goal needs more time or money than I've hinted at, tell me plainly rather than making it work on paper.
2

Audit and upgrade your skills

Your skills were written on different days, in different moods, some of them by an older model. Fable reads the whole lot in one sitting, finds the ones quietly costing you time, and rewrites the worst offenders properly.

The prompt
Go through every skill I've built, all of them, start to finish. Don't sample. For each one, tell me in plain terms: what it's meant to do, where it's letting me down (fuzzy instructions, steps that assume I'll remember something, references to files or tools that have moved or gone), and whether any two skills are stepping on each other. Then rank them from "pulling its weight" to "quietly broken". Pick the three where a fix would save me the most time each week and rewrite those three in full. Show me the before and after for anything you change so I can follow your thinking. Don't invent new skills and don't make anything cleverer than it needs to be. I maintain all of this on my own, so plain and reliable wins.
3

Run a security review on your own setup

This is the one Fable is genuinely best-in-class at, and not by accident. It is so sharp at finding security holes that access to it was restricted for a few weeks before it came back with guardrails. So point it at your own business, your keys, your webhooks, anything Claude Code has built, and let it find the gaps before anyone else does.

The prompt
I want a security review of my own setup. Everything you can see here belongs to me and I'm asking you to check it over. Go looking for: keys, passwords or tokens sitting somewhere they shouldn't be, like hardcoded in a script, saved into notes, or committed into a git repo. Webhook links or form endpoints a stranger could hit. Anything left open to the world, like a test page, a staging link, or a doc or sheet set to "anyone with the link". Connections and permissions broader than the job needs. And any automation that would cost me money or embarrass me if the wrong person set it off. For each thing you find, give me the risk in one plain sentence, how likely it really is, how much damage it could do, and the exact fix. Group it into "sort today", "sort this week", and "worth doing eventually". Report it all back first. Don't change a single thing until I've read it and said go.
4

Audit your whole setup, then fix it

Memory, instruction files, skills, connections, the folders that quietly pile up. Fable holds all of it in its head at once, hands you a ranked list of what to fix, then works down the list with you instead of leaving you a pile of homework.

The prompt
Put your consultant hat on and look at my whole setup, the way someone I was paying to find every improvement would. My instruction files, my memory, my skills, my connected tools, my folders, and how my business runs day to day as far as you can see it. Give me four things: what's working that I should lean on harder; what's redundant or arguing with itself (duplicate instructions, stale memory, skills that overlap); what's missing that someone running a business like mine would obviously want; and the single highest-impact change if I could only make one. Name the actual files and be specific. Hand me the full list first, sorted by impact. Then work down it with me one item at a time, making each fix and showing me what changed before you move on. Assume I look after all of this myself with no developer. If I won't understand a change in six months, it's the wrong change.
