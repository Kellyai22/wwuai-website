Micro sophistications

These aren't flashy AI tricks. They're the small, considered ways to use Claude that make your work sharper, your decisions clearer and your communication stronger.

Communication 2 minutes

The email second opinion

Before you hit send on anything important, get a sceptical read. Claude catches the things you're too close to see.

"I'm about to send this email to [describe the person and context]. What would a sceptical reader push back on? Be specific."

✓ Fewer regretful sends, better first impressions

Confidence 3 minutes

The brand voice check

Before you post, make sure it sounds like you. Not like a template. Not like AI. Like you on a good day.

"Here's something I'm about to post. Does it sound like a real person wrote it? Flag anything that feels generic, stiff or like it could have come from anyone. Suggest specific edits that would make it sound more like a warm, confident business owner."

✓ Everything you publish sounds unmistakably like you

Decisions 5 minutes

The proposal stress-test

Before you send a proposal or pitch, let Claude poke holes in it so your client doesn't have to.

"I'm about to send this proposal to [describe the client and project]. Read it as if you're the client receiving it. What questions would you still have? What feels vague or risky? Where might you hesitate before saying yes?"

✓ Proposals that close because you addressed concerns before they were raised

Thinking 5 minutes

The meeting prep briefing

Walk into every meeting feeling prepared, not scrambling. A couple of minutes of prep that changes the whole dynamic.

"I have a meeting with [who] about [topic] in [timeframe]. Give me: 1) the three most important things to cover, 2) one question I should ask that shows I've thought about this, 3) anything I should watch out for or avoid saying."

✓ Walk in prepared, not winging it

Communication 3 minutes

The follow-up tone check

Following up without sounding desperate or pushy is an art. Let Claude find the right note.

"I need to follow up with [person] about [what]. It's been [how long] since my last message. Write a follow-up that's warm and professional but doesn't sound desperate or passive-aggressive. Keep it short."

✓ Follow-ups that get replies, not eye rolls

Decisions 5 minutes

The decision sounding board

When you're going in circles in your own head, an outside perspective cuts through the noise.

"I'm trying to decide between [option A] and [option B]. Here's the context: [situation]. I'm leaning toward [which one] but I'm not sure. What am I probably not considering? Ask me three questions that would help me decide."

✓ Clarity without the overthinking spiral

Thinking 5 minutes

The weekly reflection

End each week knowing what actually moved your business forward, not just what kept you busy.

"Here's what I worked on this week: [list your main activities]. Help me sort these into two buckets: things that moved my business forward, and things that just kept me busy. Then suggest one thing I should do more of next week and one thing I should drop or delegate."

✓ A weekly habit that compounds into better decisions

Confidence 3 minutes

The "explain it simply" translator

When you need to explain something complex to a client, a partner, or yourself. No jargon, no confusion.

"Explain [concept or topic] to me like I'm a smart person who just hasn't encountered this before. No jargon, no dumbing it down. Use a real-world analogy if it helps."

✓ Understand anything well enough to explain it to anyone

Thinking 5 minutes

The scope check

Before you say yes to a new project, request or collaboration, make sure it actually makes sense for your business right now.

"Someone has asked me to [describe the opportunity]. Here's what I'm already working on: [list current priorities]. Help me think through whether this is worth taking on. What would I need to say no to? What's the real cost of saying yes?"

✓ Say yes with intention, not obligation

Thinking 5 minutes

The pricing gut-check

Before you set or change a price, pressure-test it. Pricing with confidence instead of anxiety.

"I'm thinking of pricing [product or service] at [amount]. My audience is [who they are] and the main outcome they get is [what they get]. Does this pricing feel right? What would make someone hesitate at this price, and what would make them feel it's worth it?"

✓ Price with logic, not just hope

Communication 3 minutes

The testimonial ask

Getting social proof from happy clients without it feeling awkward or transactional. The right words make all the difference.

"I want to ask [client name/type] for a testimonial. They were happy with [what you did for them]. Write a short, warm message asking if they'd be willing to share a few words about their experience. Make it easy for them to say yes and keep it under 100 words."

✓ Social proof that arrives because you made it easy to say yes

Communication 3 minutes

The difficult conversation prep

When you need to say something tricky, practise the conversation first. Find the words before you need them.

"I need to have a conversation with [who] about [what]. The tricky part is [why it's difficult]. Help me find the right way to say this. I want to be honest and direct but not hurtful. Give me the opening line and two ways to respond if they push back."

✓ Hard conversations that go better than you expected
