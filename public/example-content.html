A Wise Women content example

Turn one idea into
three useful drafts.

Start with your own thinking. Ask AI to prepare different drafts from that source, then check each one before using it.

This walkthrough shows the approach behind the Systems Library resource. The text below is a worked example for this page, not a member result or a measured time-saving claim.

What you need

One real idea, a clear audience, your approved facts and somewhere to save the finished instructions. Use an AI tool you already have. This example does not need an automation subscription.

The source idea

Your business shouldn't need you for everything. Choose one recurring job. Decide what needs your judgement and what can follow a repeatable process. Start small, test it on real work and improve it.

Audience: Gen X women running their own businesses.
Purpose: help someone choose a first improvement.
Boundary: no invented results, customer stories or promises that AI will run the business.

The instruction

Use only the source idea and facts above. Prepare three drafts:
1. A short social post that helps the reader recognise one repeated job.
2. An email opening that explains why that job is a useful place to start.
3. A practical tip with one small action the reader can take.
Keep the language direct and conversational. Do not invent a story, statistic, saving or offer. Flag missing information instead of filling it in.

The sample output

Draft 1 · Short post

What's the job you keep doing from scratch? The same reply, the weekly update, the content brief. Choose one and write down what starts it, what you need and what you check before it's done. That's a useful place to start building a system.

Draft 2 · Email opening

A quicker draft helps, but it doesn't solve the whole job if you still have to remember every step around it. Start with one recurring task you know well. Decide where your judgement matters, then make the other steps easier to repeat.

Draft 3 · Practical tip

Open a note and write three headings: what starts this job, what I need, and what I check. Fill them in for one task you repeat. Keep any step that needs your decision clearly marked.

The final check stays human

  • Does every claim come from the source?
  • Does the language sound like you?
  • Is each draft useful in the place you intend to publish it?
  • Are private details, promises or unverified claims missing from the final version?

Save the instruction, approved source and edited drafts together. Next time, replace the source idea and run the same checks. Nothing in this example sends or publishes automatically.

What changes?

Before: you explain the same context each time and rebuild separate drafts.
After: one source feeds three drafts, with a named review step and instructions you can reuse.

Measure the whole job if you want to judge the time saving: preparation, drafting, checking, editing and maintenance. There is no measured saving claimed for this example.
