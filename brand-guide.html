01

Logo

The WWUAI logo features a stylised "ai" lettermark with a terracotta accent dot, paired with the full brand name set beneath. Two primary versions exist for light and dark backgrounds.

Primary Versions

Primary (light backgrounds)

Dark variant (brown backgrounds)

Available files: WWUAI Logo OG.png (1000x1000, with background), WWUAI Logo OGTP.png (1024x1024, transparent), WWUAI Logo Dark.png (1024x1024, with brown background).

Usage Rules

Do

  • Use the transparent version (OGTP) when placing on brand backgrounds
  • Maintain clear space of at least the height of the terracotta dot on all sides
  • Use the dark variant on brown or dark backgrounds
  • Keep the logo at a minimum width of 80px for digital use

Don't

  • Stretch, skew, or rotate the logo
  • Change the logo colours
  • Place the logo on busy or clashing backgrounds
  • Add drop shadows, outlines, or effects
  • Recreate the logo in a different font

Buttons and CTAs

Our buttons use an outlined, minimal style. They feel approachable rather than aggressive. On hover, they fill with colour for clear interactive feedback.

On Light Backgrounds

On Dark Backgrounds

Button rules: Outlined style is the default. Use brown outlines for primary actions and terracotta for secondary or accent CTAs. Buttons use Montserrat at 500 weight with slight letter-spacing. Border radius is 6px. Hover fills the button with the border colour.

05

Imagery Direction

Our imagery should feel warm, real, and relatable. We show women in their element, working with confidence, in spaces that feel comfortable and inviting.

Think natural light streaming through a window, a woman working at her laptop with a cup of tea, cosy home offices, warm textures, and genuine expressions. Our photos should look like they belong in your life, not on a stock photo site.

Natural light Warm tones Real women Cosy workspaces Laptops and tech Genuine expressions 40+ representation Confident, not performative Soft textures Earth tones

Do

  • Use photos with warm, natural lighting
  • Show diverse women over 40 where possible
  • Feature real work environments (home offices, cafes, studios)
  • Choose images with a warm colour temperature that matches our palette
  • Use candid, natural-feeling compositions

Don't

  • Use cold, blue-toned corporate stock photos
  • Show overly polished, staged environments
  • Use images with heavy filters or unnatural colours
  • Feature only young models or exclusively tech-bro imagery
  • Use images that feel intimidating or exclusionary

06

Brand Voice and Tone

We talk to our audience like a knowledgeable friend. Someone who's been there, figured it out, and is happy to show you how, without making you feel small for not knowing already.

Voice Pillars

Warm

Friendly and approachable. We write like a real person talks, not like a press release.

Direct

Clear and straightforward. We say what we mean without padding it with filler words.

Practical

Focused on what you can actually do with this. Every piece of content should leave someone with something useful.

Encouraging

Supportive without being preachy. We believe in our audience and we show it.

Copy Rules

  • Australian and British English spelling at all times (organise, colour, realise)
  • No em dashes, ever. Use a comma, a full stop, or restructure the sentence
  • No all-caps for emphasis. Use sentence structure to carry weight instead
  • Short sentences preferred, especially for social content
  • No corporate speak. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it
  • No unnecessary jargon. If a technical term is needed, explain it simply

Writing Examples

Avoid

"Leverage our cutting-edge AI solutions to unlock your potential and take your business to the next level with our transformative, game-changing platform."

Write like this

"AI can save you hours every week. Here's how to set it up in your business, step by step, no tech skills required."

Avoid

"We're excited to announce our innovative new course that will delve deeper into the seamless integration of AI into your robust business workflows."

Write like this

"New course is live. It walks you through exactly how to use AI for the tasks you're already doing, just faster and easier."

Banned Words and Phrases

07

Quick Reference: Do's and Don'ts

A summary of the key rules across the entire brand.

Visual Identity

Do

  • Use only the six brand colours defined in the palette
  • Use Cormorant Garamond for headings, Montserrat for body
  • Use terracotta sparingly, for accents and highlights
  • Keep the logo clear and unobstructed
  • Use outlined buttons for CTAs
  • Choose warm, naturally lit photography

Don't

  • Use black, purple gradients, or off-palette colours
  • Mix in random fonts not in the type system
  • Use terracotta as a primary background colour
  • Alter, stretch, or recolour the logo
  • Use solid, heavy-looking buttons
  • Use cold, corporate, or overly staged stock imagery

Written Content

Do

  • Write in Australian/British English spelling
  • Keep sentences short and conversational
  • Explain technical concepts in plain language
  • Sound like a knowledgeable friend
  • Read copy aloud, and rewrite if it sounds stiff
  • Use "use" instead of "utilise"

Don't

  • Use em dashes
  • Write in ALL CAPS for emphasis
  • Use any words from the banned list
  • Sound corporate, preachy, or condescending
  • Over-explain or pad content with filler
  • Write anything that sounds like a LinkedIn thought leader post
