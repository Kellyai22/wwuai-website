Every prompt from the demo

These are the exact prompts I used to turn one of Jo's newsletters into five complete Instagram posts. Same order I ran them in, copy and paste straight across.

A quick word before you start: these are starting points, not magic spells. They worked because Jo's Claude already knew her voice, her audience and her business. Tune each one to yours and the output gets sharper every time. Swap Jo's details for your own, change the angles to suit what you do, and make them sound like you.

1

The content plan

Start here. Upload your source (a newsletter, a podcast, a voice note) and ask for the shape of the week before any drafting. You want to see the five angles first.

Attached is the newsletter Jo wrote this week. I want to turn this into five Instagram posts for her, each approaching the same core idea from a different angle. The thinking, the voice, and the story are already in the newsletter. I want you to use them as the source. Don't invent new examples. Don't rewrite her voice. Take what she's already said and spread it across five posts that each do a different job. The five angles: 1. The moment itself. First-person story, intimate, no big lesson. 2. The bigger principle. What this moment actually was, why it matters in this season of life. 3. A question for the audience. Engagement-led, invites comments. 4. A client story. Adapt or expand on anything in the newsletter that hints at a client experience. 5. CTA post. Inviting people to work with her, soft sell in her voice. Give me the content plan first, just the angle and one-line summary for each, before we write any of them. I want to see the shape before we start drafting.

Then, the human edit

When Claude refused to invent a client story (because Jo's setup tells it never to fabricate one), I re-angled that post instead of forcing it. This is the prompt I used to change the plan. Your version will have its own moments like this, where you steer rather than accept.

Drop the client story for Post 4, you're right not to invent one. Post 4 is now a permission post. Update the plan only, don't draft any posts yet: give Jo's audience permission to reclaim one small joy of their own this week, no productivity attached, no guilt. Build it from the invitation in her newsletter (find one moment this week). Change only the one-line plan entry for Post 4. Leave Posts 1, 2, 3 and 5 exactly as they are. Don't write, draft, or summarise any posts, and don't tell me anything is "done." Just show me the updated plan and wait for me to ask for the drafts.
2

Post one, the story

The moment itself. First person, intimate, no big lesson at the end. Notice the output format you ask for: hook, caption, hashtags, visual direction, CTA. That is what turns a caption into a complete post.

Let's write post 1. The story of the moment in the car. First person, in Jo's voice, pulled directly from the newsletter. Intimate. Specific. No big lesson at the end. Just the moment, told well. Caption should feel like Jo's writing in her journal, not lecturing. Give me: - Hook (first line, the scroll-stopper) - Full caption - 3-5 hashtags, specific phrases not generic single words - Visual direction (specific enough Jo can execute or hand to Canva) - CTA
3

Post two, the principle

Same idea, zoomed out. This one is allowed to teach a little. Keep the output format identical so every post arrives ready to schedule.

Post 2. Same core idea, small joy returning, but now we're zooming out. What's actually happening in that car moment, and why does it matter in this season of life? Tie it to perimenopause or midlife if it fits naturally. Losing access to your own joy is a real symptom, not just a metaphor. Pull from the newsletter wherever the thinking is already there. Same output format: hook, caption, hashtags, visual direction, CTA. This one's allowed to teach a little. Jo's audience trusts her, they want her thinking.
4

Posts three, four and five

Once you have the rhythm, batch the rest. Three posts in one go: the question, the permission post and the soft CTA, each with the same five-part format.

Now post 3, 4, and 5. Post 3. The audience question. When did you last sing along to something just for you? Or a variation. Make it specific and answerable, not a vague 'tell me your thoughts.' The job is to start conversations in the comments. Post 4. The permission post, as per the updated plan. Give Jo's audience permission to reclaim one small joy of their own this week, no productivity attached, no guilt. Build it from the invitation in her newsletter ('find one moment this week'). Warm, direct, ends with a gentle nudge to act, not a lesson. Post 5. The CTA post. A soft invitation to work with Jo 1:1. No hype, no scarcity, no pressure. Just a clear 'this is what I do and here's how to find me.' Jo's voice. Same output format for each: hook, caption, hashtags, visual direction, CTA.

You don't need more ideas

You need to do more with the ones you already have. That is the whole point of the system. One good thought, properly spread across a week.
