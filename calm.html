How it works

Why this gets the load off you

The reason it all lives in your head is that it has nowhere else to go. You're the plan, the memory and the backup, all at once. These four prompts do it in order: first they empty your head onto the page, then they sort the pile, then they shape a real week, then they show you what only you can do and what you can finally hand off. Do them one after the other on a Sunday, or any evening the week feels like too much. By the end, the week is written down and out of you, and you can look at it instead of carrying it.

The reset

The four prompts, in order

Paste each one in, answer honestly, and let it work before you move to the next.

1

Empty your head onto the page

Don't sort yet. Just get it all out, work and home tangled together, exactly as it lives in your mind. The point is to stop holding it, not to organise it. That comes next.

Prompt one
I'm going to empty my head out to you. It's going to be messy and out of order, business and home all mixed together, and that's fine. I just need to stop carrying it. Here's everything on my mind for the week ahead: [start typing, no order, no filter. Deadlines, the thing you keep forgetting, who you owe a reply to, the appointment, the invoice, what's for dinner, the half-finished idea, all of it.] Don't do anything with it yet. Just read it all back to me grouped into loose buckets so I can see I've got it out, and gently ask me what I've left out, the things that tend to hide until 3am.
2

Sort the pile

Now let it do the sorting you don't have the headspace for. What actually matters this week, what only feels urgent, and what can quietly wait without anything falling over.

Prompt two
Take everything I just gave you and sort it into four honest groups: matters this week, matters but not this week, someone else could do this, and this can quietly drop off. Be firm with me. I tend to treat everything as urgent because it's all sitting on me, so push back where something is loud but not actually important. For anything you put in "someone else could do this", say who or what could take it, even if that's a tool, a template, or a five-minute ask to another person. Give it back to me as four short lists, nothing else.
3

Shape a week that fits a real person

Not a fantasy schedule that assumes nothing goes sideways. A week with room in it, built around your actual energy, so Monday morning already has a shape before you open your laptop.

Prompt three
Using only the "matters this week" list, help me lay the week out so it fits a real person, not a machine. A few things about me: [when your brain is sharpest, when you're wrung out, any fixed commitments, appointments, the standing catch-ups, the days that are already heavy.] Give me a simple day-by-day plan. Put the hard-thinking work where my energy is best. Leave genuine gaps for the things that always come up. Don't stack the week so tightly that one late reply topples the lot. Keep it to a page I can actually glance at, and tell me the one thing that, if I only did that this week, would make the biggest difference.
4

See what only you can do

This is the one that starts to change things. The whole reason it's all on you is that no one's ever drawn the line between what genuinely needs you and what just ended up with you. This draws it.

Prompt four
Look at my whole week and everything I emptied out earlier. I've been the only one holding all of it, and I want to start changing that. Split it into two honest lists: the things that genuinely need me, my judgement, my relationships, my voice, and the things that only landed on me because there was no one and nothing else to catch them. For that second list, give me the very first thing I could hand off this week, and exactly how. Whether that's a prompt I could reuse, a simple checklist someone else could follow, or a task a tool could take. Start me with one. Small enough that I'll actually do it, real enough that I feel the weight come off.
