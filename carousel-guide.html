03

Setting up your brand

The first time you run the carousel maker, a setup wizard walks you through a series of questions. Your answers get saved to a brand-config.json file, and every carousel after that automatically uses your brand settings.

1

Brand name and signature name

Your business name for cover slides, and the name that appears on the final slide sign-off. These can be different — "Wise Women Use AI" as the brand, "Kelly" as the signature.

2

Instagram handle

Your @handle — it appears on every content slide as subtle attribution. Include the @ symbol.

3

Brand colours — 5 hex values

Primary (main colour, used for overlays), secondary (accent — CTAs and highlights), accent (softer tone), background (lightest colour — near-white works best), and text (muted body copy colour). These map directly to the CSS variables in your template.

4

Headline and body fonts

Pick two Google Fonts — one for headlines, one for body text. Go to fonts.google.com, select your font, and copy the @import URL when the wizard asks for it. If you're unsure, Cormorant Garamond (headlines) and Montserrat (body) is a solid warm pairing.

5

Drop your photos first

Before this step, put 3–5 brand photos into the photos/my-brand/ folder inside the skill directory. The wizard checks the folder and confirms how many it found. More photos means more variety across carousels.

6

ManyChat keyword and CTA text

The keyword people comment to get your link (e.g. CAROUSEL) and the tail text that follows it on the final slide (e.g. "and I'll send you the link."). This becomes your CTA slide automatically on every carousel.

7

Banned phrases

Words or phrases you never want in your copy — "game-changer", "unlock", "hustle", whatever you hate. Claude checks every slide against this list before showing you the draft. Say "none" if you don't have any yet.

What the config file looks like after setup

{

}

You can edit this file directly at any time, or tell Claude "change my primary colour to #2a4a5a" and it'll update automatically.

04

How the pipeline works

The carousel maker runs a five-stage process. You interact with the first two stages. Everything after your approval is automatic.

1

You give it a topic

A topic, an angle, a transcript, or even pre-written copy. "Create a carousel about why you don't need a content calendar" is all it needs to get started.

2

Copy generation — then your approval

Claude writes 7 slides in your brand voice, checking against your banned phrases. You review, make any edits, then say "looks good." Nothing renders until you approve.

3

HTML build

Your approved copy gets injected into your brand template. Colours, fonts, photos, handle, and CTA all come from your brand config automatically — no manual work.

4

PNG export

Claude opens the HTML in a headless browser and screenshots each of the 7 slides at 1080×1350px — Instagram-ready, 4:5 aspect ratio, sharp on every device.

5

Files land in a folder

Seven PNGs are saved and the folder opens in Finder automatically. Ready to drag straight into your scheduling tool or upload directly to Instagram.

The approval gate is by design

The skill always pauses after writing copy and waits for your sign-off. This isn't a limitation — it's the most important design decision. Your voice matters more than speed. Tweak a headline, swap a word, reorder a slide. Then say "looks good" and the rest is automatic.

05

Your first carousel

Once you're set up, this is the only command you need.

First-time setup (run once)

setup carousel maker

Every carousel after that

create a carousel about [your topic]

The first run takes about 20 minutes because you're configuring your brand. Every carousel after that takes under two minutes from prompt to seven finished PNGs.

Some topics that work well to start with:

create a carousel about why you don't need a content calendar
create a carousel about the three tools I use every single morning
create a carousel about what changed when I stopped batch-creating content

If your first result doesn't look right

It's almost always one of two things: photos that are too dark or low-contrast, or brand colours without enough difference between them. Try lighter, more open photos. Or adjust your background colour to be closer to white. You can re-run setup at any time without losing your other settings.

06

The italic emphasis

Every carousel headline has exactly one italicised word. This isn't a setting you configure — it's baked into the skill. And it's the thing that makes carousels look considered rather than generated.

The italic word is the punchline — the one that carries the most weight or surprise in the sentence. The skill chooses it carefully when writing your copy, but you can always change it at the approval stage by saying which word you'd like italicised instead.

Without

"You don't need to think bigger."

With italic emphasis

"You don't need to think bigger."

Without

"Sharper beats bigger."

With italic emphasis

"Sharper beats bigger."

Without

"The advice was written for someone else."

With italic emphasis

"The advice was written for someone else."

At the approval stage, look at the italic word on each slide and ask: is this the right one? If another word carries more punch, just tell Claude which word to italicise. It'll update the slide before rendering.

Getting it running

Three steps from download to your first setup prompt.

1

Download and unzip the file

Use the button below (or the link in your inbox). Unzip it — you'll get a folder called carousel-maker.

2

Move it to your Claude Code skills folder

Drop the entire carousel-maker folder into ~/.claude/skills/ — that's the hidden .claude folder in your home directory. If the skills/ folder doesn't exist yet, create it first.

3

Open Claude Code and run setup

Open the Claude Code desktop app, start a new session, and type setup carousel maker. The wizard starts automatically. About 20 minutes the first time, then you're ready to go.
