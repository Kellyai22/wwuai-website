01

What Claude can take off your plate

The interview that finds your first hand-overs. Run it once, keep the list.

You are my warm, practical business helper. I run my own business and I am stretched thin on time and energy. I want your help working out which tasks I could hand to AI so I can get some of my head back. Please interview me first. Ask me one question at a time, wait for my answer, and keep it conversational and friendly. Do not lecture me. Cover, roughly in order: what my business actually is in plain terms; a normal week and what I spend the most time on; the parts of the work I genuinely enjoy and want to keep doing myself; the parts I dread, put off, or find draining; the small repetitive jobs that eat my time without me noticing; and where I most often feel behind or overwhelmed. Ask follow-up questions if my answer is vague. Once you have enough to go on, stop interviewing and give me: my top five tasks you could take off my plate, ordered by how much relief they'd give me for the least effort to set up; for each one, a single sentence on how you'd help and what I'd keep doing myself; and a clear start-here pick, the one task to hand over first this week. Keep it warm and encouraging. I am not a tech person, so no jargon. Talk to me like a smart friend who has done this before.
02

Your five ready-made replies

Write the answers to your most-asked questions once, reuse them forever.

I run my own business selling [what you sell] and I get asked the same questions again and again. Here are my five most common ones: 1. [question 1] 2. [question 2] 3. [question 3] 4. [question 4] 5. [question 5] Please write a warm, clear reply for each one that I can reuse in emails, DMs and enquiries. Keep each reply short, friendly and practical, the way a real person writes. No jargon, no stiff formal openings. After you've drafted them, ask me if any need to sound more like me, and help me adjust until they do.
03

This week's content, without the blank page

One clear message, shared a few ways. No staring at the ceiling.

Help me create this week's content for my small business. My audience is [who you sell to]. The main problem on their mind this week is [the problem]. Please create: one short social post, one longer caption with a bit more story in it, and one short email to my list. Keep the tone warm, clear and practical, like a knowledgeable friend. Focus on helping, not selling. When you're done, ask me two quick questions that would make next week's batch even more like me.
04

The idea generator

A short list, a clear pick, and on with your day.

I run a small business selling [what you sell]. My audience is [who you sell to]. Help me work out what to focus on next. Please give me: three content ideas, two email ideas, and one simple offer or promotion I could run this fortnight. Base everything on my audience's real problems and priorities, not trends. Then, and this is the important part, tell me which one of those you'd pick first and why, in plain language.
05

Your one AI hour a week

The weekly session that batches everything and quietens your head.

Help me run my weekly business hour. I have [30/45/60] minutes. In that time I want to: plan my priorities for the week, prepare this week's content, tidy up any customer replies I need, and finish with a clear picture of what matters most. Please walk me through it step by step, one thing at a time, and keep me moving. If I start overthinking something, gently move me along. At the end, give me my week on a few lines I can screenshot.
